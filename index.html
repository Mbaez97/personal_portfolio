01 — About

Between the method and the people who use it

I am a software engineer who moved into research, and I have kept both halves. I spent six years designing and building complex production systems before starting a PhD in Applied Mathematics and Data Science at FGV EMAp in Rio de Janeiro, where I now work on machine learning for biological networks in Alberto Paccanaro's group.

That combination shapes what I choose to work on. A clustering algorithm that only its author can run is not finished; neither is a web tool with no method behind it. My published work so far sits exactly on that seam — taking an established algorithm for finding overlapping protein complexes and turning it into something a biologist can open in a browser and get an answer from.

I like working in teams, I am comfortable leading one, and I do my best work under a deadline. I grew up and trained as an engineer in Paraguay, and I am now based in Brazil.

02 — Research

What I am working on

Proteins do their work in groups, and most of those groups are still uncharacterised. My research uses the structure of interaction networks — plus what sequence models already know — to predict what a protein does and which complex it belongs to.

Protein function prediction

Label propagation over biological networks, set against what protein language models already infer from sequence alone. The question underneath: when does network structure still carry information a sequence model misses, and for which organisms? This is the core of my doctoral thesis, in progress.

Graph MLProtein language modelsBenchmarking

Overlapping protein complexes

Proteins belong to more than one complex at a time, so the clustering has to overlap. ClusterONE detects those communities in weighted and unweighted PPI networks; my work brought it to the web with curated multi-organism datasets and Gene Ontology enrichment built in.

03 — Publications

Peer-reviewed work

  1. 2025

    ClusterONE Web: a tool for discovering and analyzing overlapping protein complexes

    Marcelo Baez, Ruben Jimenez, Luca Cernuzzi, Alberto Paccanaro

    Nucleic Acids Research Vol. 53, Issue W1, pages W172–W177 · Web Server Issue · July 2025

    ClusterONE detects overlapping protein complexes in weighted and unweighted protein–protein interaction networks. We built a free, platform-independent web version: a database of preprocessed PPI datasets across multiple organisms, support for user-uploaded interaction data, an interactive view of the detected complexes, and Gene Ontology enrichment analysis for interpreting them — with nothing to install.

ClusterONE Web

A browser-based platform for detecting overlapping protein complexes in PPI networks. Ships with preprocessed datasets for multiple organisms, accepts your own interaction data, renders the detected complexes interactively and runs Gene Ontology enrichment on them.
